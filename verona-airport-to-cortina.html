Dolomites

Verona Airport to Cortina d'Ampezzo

About 260 kilometres and three hours of mountain road to a resort with no railway station. Here is what each way in actually involves.

The short version

Route at a glance

  • Route: Verona Villafranca Airport (VRN) → Cortina d'Ampezzo
  • Distance: about 260 km
  • Driving time: around 3 hours
  • Price from: roughly 800 USD per vehicle, not per person

Cortina is the most expensive transfer on this site by a wide margin, and the reason is simple: it is a long way from anywhere with a runway, and there is no train.

Cortina has no railway station

This catches people out every winter. The line that once served Cortina closed decades ago and is now a cycle path. The nearest station is Calalzo-Pieve di Cadore, roughly half an hour further down the valley by bus, and getting to Calalzo from Verona means Venice first and then a slow regional line up through the Cadore.

So the realistic options are a bus, a transfer, or a hire car.

How else you can get there

By bus from Venice, not Verona

If you are set on public transport, fly into Venice rather than Verona. Coach services run from Venice airport and Mestre up to Cortina in roughly three hours and are by far the cheapest way in. They run to a timetable that thins out sharply in the evening and off-season, and they take you to the bus terminal in Cortina, not to your hotel — which, in a resort spread along a valley, can mean a final taxi anyway.

By hire car

Worth it if you plan to drive the passes — Falzarego, Giau, Tre Croci are the reason many people come. Worth considerably less if you are here to ski: Cortina parking is tight in season, and winter tyres or chains are a legal requirement on these roads.

By private transfer

Door to door, fixed price, no changes with ski bags, and a driver who is tracking your flight. In a vehicle for four or six, the per-person cost stops looking absurd.

Be honest about the price

For two people, a transfer from Verona to Cortina is poor value next to flying into Venice and taking the coach. We would not pretend otherwise.

For a family of four, a group of six, or anyone arriving late with ski equipment, it is a different calculation entirely — one fixed price for the car, no timetable, no transfers, and the driver waits if the flight is late.

Arriving in winter

Two things are worth planning around. Saturday is changeover day across the Dolomites, and the valley roads queue accordingly; a midweek arrival is a noticeably different experience. And the last hour of the drive is mountain road — in snow, allow more than the three hours the map promises, and do not book anything tight for the evening you land.

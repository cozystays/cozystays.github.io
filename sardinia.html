Region guide

Sardinia

People come for the water, and the water deserves it. What surprises them is the inland — empty roads, stone villages and a language that is not Italian.

Which coast to base yourself on

Sardinia is long and the coasts are genuinely different. Pick one and explore it properly rather than circling the island.

The northeast, for the famous beaches

Olbia is the airport and the ferry port, and the Costa Smeralda starts just north of it. The water here is the postcard — pale sand, granite boulders, impossible turquoise. It is also the most expensive corner of the island in August. San Teodoro and the Golfo Aranci side are the same sea for less money.

Alghero, for the old town

On the northwest coast, with a walled seafront old town that was Catalan for three centuries and still has the street signs to prove it. The most characterful base on the island, its own airport, and the cliffs at Capo Caccia nearby.

Cagliari, for the south

The capital, built up a hill, with a long city beach at Poetto and the wilder southwest coast within reach. Warmer later in the year than the north and much quieter in the shoulder season.

The Golfo di Orosei, for the dramatic bit

The east coast between Orosei and Baunei is where the limestone drops straight into the sea. Several of the best coves here have no road at all and are reached only by boat or on foot. Base in Cala Gonone or Dorgali.

Getting there and getting around

Olbia, Alghero or Cagliari

Three airports, one for each corner. Match the airport to the coast you picked — driving from Olbia to Cagliari takes most of a day and is not a scenic highlight. Ferries from the mainland take vehicles if you would rather bring your own car.

A car is not optional here

Sardinia's beaches are the whole point, and almost none of them have a bus. Many of the best ones sit at the end of an unmarked track through scrub, several kilometres from the nearest village. The inland villages are worse served again.

The good news is that driving here is easy — the roads are empty by Italian standards, well surfaced and often beautiful in themselves. Some beach access roads are gravel and a few charge for parking in summer.

Book early for July and August. Sardinia runs out of rental cars in high season more reliably than anywhere else in Italy, and the prices at the last minute are punishing.

Booking the practical bits

  • Rental cars at Olbia — pickup at the airport for the Costa Smeralda and the northeast. Free cancellation on many, and summer sells out early.
  • Compare fares to Olbia, Alghero and Cagliari — the three airports differ a lot in price depending on the month.
  • eSIM for Italy — half the good beaches are found by map rather than by sign.

Can you visit Sardinia without a car?

Honestly: less well than anywhere else in these guides. It is possible, but you are choosing a town holiday on an island famous for its coastline.

What exists

Buses connect the main towns, and a slow narrow-gauge line crosses part of the interior. Cagliari and Alghero both work as walkable bases with a city or town beach you can reach without driving — Poetto in Cagliari is served by city buses, and Alghero's old town is compact. In July and August seasonal buses run to some of the bigger beaches from Olbia and Cagliari, but they thin out or vanish the rest of the year.

What you will miss

Nearly all of it. The coves of the Golfo di Orosei, the Costa Smeralda's smaller beaches, the inland villages and the nuraghe sites are not served. Boat trips are the one genuine workaround on the east coast — several of the best coves are reached by sea anyway, so a boat day from Cala Gonone costs you nothing in access.

If you are not driving

Base in Alghero or Cagliari, use boat excursions for the coast, and accept it as a town-and-sea trip. Or rent for two or three days only, which is what most people end up doing.

Worth your time

A boat day in the Golfo di Orosei. Cala Goloritzé, Cala Mariolu and the rest are reached by sea, and the cliffs look nothing like the rest of the Mediterranean.

Capo Caccia and the Neptune's Grotto stairs near Alghero — several hundred steps cut into a cliff face, and the view back along them is the real attraction.

Su Nuraxi at Barumini. A bronze-age stone tower complex older than most of what people fly to Italy to see, and almost nobody goes.

June or September. The sea is warm, the beaches have room, and the car hire still exists.

Before you go

For summer, book the car first and the room second — that is the right order on this island. An eSIM and travel cover take a few minutes each before you fly.

Region guide

Puglia

The heel of Italy: olive groves to the horizon, whitewashed hill towns, and a coast that still feels unfinished. Cheaper than the north and emptier outside August.

Where to base yourself

Puglia is long. Trying to see all of it from one base means a lot of driving — most people are happier splitting a week between two.

Valle d'Itria, for the trulli country

The valley between Alberobello, Locorotondo, Cisternino and Martina Franca is the heart of the region: dry-stone huts with conical roofs scattered through olive groves, and hill towns of narrow white lanes. Staying in a converted trullo or a masseria — an old fortified farmhouse, many now small hotels — is the reason to come.

Ostuni, for the view and the sea

A white town piled on a hill above the olive plain, with beaches twenty minutes away. Busier than the valley towns and correspondingly better supplied with restaurants.

Lecce, for the south

A baroque city in soft golden stone, small enough to walk, and the natural base for the Salento peninsula and its two coasts. Roughly an hour and a half from the Valle d'Itria.

Polignano a Mare, for a short trip

Built on a cliff over the sea, close to Bari airport, good for a first or last night. Very busy in summer for its size.

Getting there and getting around

Bari or Brindisi

Bari is the bigger airport and better for the Valle d'Itria and the north. Brindisi is closer to Lecce and the Salento. Both are served by budget carriers from across Europe.

You will want a car

More than anywhere else in these guides. Trains connect the larger towns on slow regional lines, but the masserie, the beaches and the smaller villages are all off that network, often down unmarked lanes through olive groves. A week in Puglia without a car means seeing a fraction of it.

Driving here is easy by Italian standards — flat, quiet roads, and parking that actually exists. The usual ZTL rule applies in historic centres: park outside and walk in.

Booking the practical bits

  • Car rental in Puglia — local suppliers, pickup at Bari or Brindisi airport.
  • Private transfer Bari airport → Polignano a Mare — about 1 h 10 and 63 km. Good first or last night if you are picking up a rental car later in the trip.
  • Private transfer Bari airport → Alberobello — if you are collecting a car later or arriving late.
  • Alberobello walking tour — the trulli district with someone who can explain how the roofs were built and why.
  • Left luggage in Bari — 40 drop-off points from about €1.60 a bag a day, for the hours between dropping the car and the flight.

Worth your time

Alberobello early or late. The Rione Monti district is genuinely remarkable and genuinely mobbed between ten and four. Go at eight, or stay the night and walk it after the coaches leave.

Locorotondo and Cisternino for the evening. Smaller, less visited, and the better places to eat — Cisternino's butchers grill what you pick at the counter.

Matera, just over the border in Basilicata and an easy day trip: a city of cave dwellings carved into a ravine, abandoned within living memory and now inhabited again.

The Adriatic side in the morning, the Ionian in the afternoon. In the Salento the two coasts are half an hour apart and the water behaves completely differently.

Puglia without a car: what is realistic

The honest answer is that this is the hardest region in these guides to do without driving — but it is not impossible, and the difference between a frustrating trip and a good one is entirely about which bases you pick.

The main railway line is genuinely good

Trenitalia runs down the Adriatic coast through Bari, Monopoli, Polignano a Mare, Brindisi and on to Lecce. It is fast, frequent and cheap. Everything on that line is comfortably reachable without a car, which already gives you the sea towns and the baroque south.

The trulli country runs on a different railway

Alberobello, Locorotondo and Martina Franca are served by Ferrovie del Sud Est, a separate regional line from Bari through Putignano. It works, but set your expectations: it is slow, departures are sparse, and the service is heavily reduced at weekends and on holidays. Treat it as two or three usable departures a day rather than a turn-up-and-go line.

What is off the network entirely

The masserie — the converted farmhouses that are the best places to stay here — sit down unmarked lanes through olive groves, often several kilometres from anything. So do most of the good beaches. No bus goes there, and taxis in rural Puglia are a phone call rather than a rank.

A week that works without driving

Split it: three or four nights in Polignano a Mare or Monopoli for the coast and the day trips, then three in Lecce for the south, both on the main line. Add Alberobello or Locorotondo as a two-night stop reached by the Sud Est line, staying in the town rather than at a masseria outside it. You will miss the empty coves and the countryside stays, but you will see the region.

Or rent for two days only

The compromise most people end up happy with: do the train-served towns without a car, then pick up a rental for the two days you want the masserie, the Valle d'Itria back roads and the beaches. Driving in Puglia is easy by Italian standards — flat, quiet roads and parking that actually exists — so a short rental is far less daunting here than on the Amalfi Coast.

Travelling with children

Puglia gives you space, which is the thing that matters most. Masserie — old fortified farmhouses, many now small hotels — have grounds to run in and a pool more often than not, and a converted trullo with its conical stone roof needs no explaining to a child.

The beaches help too: sandy and shallow on much of the Adriatic side, unlike the pebble coves further north. And the driving here is easy by Italian standards — flat, quiet roads and parking that actually exists — so the car you need anyway is far less of a burden than it would be on the Amalfi Coast.

Before you go

Book a masseria well ahead for June to September; the good ones are small and go early. An eSIM matters here more than most places — you will be navigating unmarked farm lanes. Travel cover is the other pre-flight job.

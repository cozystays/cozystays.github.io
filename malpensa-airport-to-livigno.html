Malpensa Airport to Livigno & Bormio

The longest transfer on this site, through passes that do not care what your map said. What the drive involves and what the alternatives really cost you in time.

The short version

Route at a glance

  • Route: Milan Malpensa (MXP) → Livigno
  • Distance: about 227 km
  • Driving time: roughly 3.5 to 4 hours
  • Price from: around 475 USD per vehicle, not per person

Bormio is the other half of this corner: around 245 km from Malpensa and closer to five hours, because the road climbs further. Both are long days, and both are the routes where public transport performs worst in the whole country.

Why this one is genuinely awkward

The distance understates it. The last part of either drive is mountain road over passes, at altitude, in winter — and a map that says three hours is quoting summer conditions on dry tarmac. Snow, a closed pass or a queue at a tunnel portal all add time, and none of them are unusual between December and March.

There is no station in Livigno. The nearest railhead is Tirano, where the line from Milan ends, and from Tirano it is still a bus over the mountains. Planned carefully it works; planned on the day of a late flight it does not.

How else you can get there

Train to Tirano, then bus

Malpensa Express into Milan, train out to Tirano, bus up. Cheap, slow, and entirely dependent on the last bus of the day still running. For Bormio the same applies with a shorter final leg.

Hire car

A real option here, unlike in Val Gardena, because the area is spread out. The caveat is winter equipment: snow tyres or chains are required on these roads in season, and you should check what the rental actually includes rather than assuming.

Private transfer

The case for it is strongest on exactly this route — longest drive, worst public transport, most equipment, and a destination where arriving at midnight with no onward bus is a real possibility.

Livigno is duty-free

A genuine quirk worth knowing before you plan the trip: Livigno sits outside the EU customs and VAT area, a status it has held for centuries. Fuel, alcohol, tobacco and sports equipment are noticeably cheaper there than in the rest of Italy, and there are allowance limits on what you can carry back out. It is also the reason the resort feels more commercial than its neighbours.

Booking the practical bits

  • Private transfer Malpensa → Livigno — fixed price, driver at arrivals, 90 minutes of free waiting.
  • Private transfer Malpensa → Bormio — the longer run, and the spa town at the end of it.
  • An eSIM for Italy — coverage on the passes is patchy, so set it up before you land rather than on the way.
  • If the flight is delayed or cancelled.

When it is not worth it

If you are two people, arriving midday, staying a week and not skiing, the train to Tirano plus the bus is a perfectly pleasant half-day and a fraction of the price. The transfer earns its money on a group, on equipment, and on any arrival after dark.

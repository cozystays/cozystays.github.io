Region guide

Dolomites

Val Gardena, Alta Badia and Cortina in winter — which valley to pick, which airport to fly into, and how to get there without driving a mountain pass yourself.

Which valley to stay in

The Dolomites are not one place. They are a cluster of valleys separated by passes, and the pass between two of them can be closed or slow in winter. Pick the valley first, then the village. Moving between valleys mid-trip costs you half a day.

Val Gardena

Three villages — Ortisei, Santa Cristina and Selva — strung along one valley floor, linked by a frequent local bus. The easiest choice if it is your first time: everything is walkable, the lifts start from the villages themselves, and you are on the Sellaronda circuit. Ortisei is the prettiest and the busiest, Selva the most practical for skiing.

Alta Badia

Quieter than Val Gardena, with Corvara and San Cassiano as the main bases. Same lift network, fewer day visitors, a little harder to reach. Worth it if you have been before and found Val Gardena crowded.

Cortina d'Ampezzo

A town rather than a village, with shops, restaurants and a long winter season. It sits in its own basin east of the other valleys, so treat it as a separate trip, not a day out from Val Gardena.

Val di Fassa

Canazei and Campitello sit on the south side of the Sella massif. Cheaper than Val Gardena for similar access, and the usual choice when the Gardena hotels are full.

Getting there and getting around

Which airport

Innsbruck is the closest airport to Val Gardena, roughly 121 km and about 1 hour 40 minutes by road. Verona Villafranca is further — around 190 km and about 2 hours 30 minutes — but it has considerably more winter flights, which usually decides it. Venice, Bergamo, Munich and Bolzano are all used as well, with longer drives.

The last hour is the slow part

Distances here are misleading. The motorway section goes quickly, the valley roads do not, and snow or weekend traffic can add an hour on a Saturday in February. Allow a buffer on the way back to the airport rather than on the way in.

Once you are in the valley

You do not need a car to ski. Each valley runs a local ski bus between the villages and the lift stations, and the lift network itself is how you move around during the day. A car mostly becomes something to dig out in the morning.

Booking the practical bits

  • Verona airport to Selva di Val Gardena — about 190 km, roughly 2 h 30, fixed price, door to door. Note skis or a snowboard in the booking comment so a van is assigned.
  • Bolzano to Cortina d'Ampezzo — about 130 km across the passes, roughly 2 h 35.
  • Transfers from the other airports — Innsbruck, Venice, Bergamo and Munich into the valleys.

The Dolomites without a car

This is the one question worth answering properly, because the honest answer is yes — and it is often the better trip.

A transfer replaces the drive in

A private transfer is a fixed price agreed before you travel, the driver meets you in the arrivals hall, and you are dropped at the hotel door rather than at a bus stop with ski bags. Waiting time of up to about an hour and a half is included, so a delayed flight does not cost you the booking.

The ski bus is the local transport

Within each valley the ski bus runs between the villages and the lift bases through the season, and in most resorts it is included with your lift pass or guest card. It is how residents get around, not a tourist shuttle.

No winter tyre problem

From 15 November to 15 April, roads here require winter tyres or snow chains carried in the car. Fines run from about 41 euro in town to 335 euro on motorways and extra-urban roads, and the vehicle can be held until it is properly equipped. A rental car means checking what the company actually fitted; a transfer means the question does not arise.

When you do want a car

If you are coming in summer for the high passes, or staying somewhere with no lift access, a car earns its keep. In winter, for a valley base, it mostly sits in a hotel car park.

Travelling with children

Val Gardena is the easiest valley with small children: the villages are flat along the valley floor, the nursery slopes sit at the edge of the villages rather than up a mountain, and you can walk to them. Ortisei has the shortest distances between hotel, lift and shops.

For the journey itself, the transfer matters more than usual. Two and a half hours from Verona in a booked vehicle with child seats requested at booking is a different experience from the same two and a half hours spent collecting a rental car, fitting seats and finding the valley in the dark. Child seats are available for all ages but are generally a paid extra, so ask when you book rather than at the airport.

Before you go

Lift passes are cheaper bought for consecutive days than day by day, and the valley you choose determines which pass makes sense. Check whether your accommodation includes a guest card, because in several valleys that is what makes the ski bus free. An eSIM is worth it if you are crossing between Italy and Austria on the way in. Travel cover for winter sports is usually a separate tier from standard cover — check that the policy names skiing explicitly.

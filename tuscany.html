Region guide

Tuscany

The part people remember is not in the cities. It is the hour between them — cypress avenues, a farmhouse on a ridge, a town of six hundred people with one good restaurant.

Where to base yourself

Tuscany rewards staying put. Pick one base, unpack, and drive out from there — the distances are short and the roads are the point.

Val d'Orcia, for the landscape

South of Siena, this is the Tuscany of the postcards: rolling wheat hills, lone cypresses on ridgelines, and small walled towns. Pienza, Montalcino and San Quirico d'Orcia all make good bases, each small enough to walk in ten minutes. Agriturismi — working farms that rent rooms — are common here and often cheaper than hotels in Florence, with a better view.

Chianti, for the compromise

Between Florence and Siena, close enough to visit both as day trips without staying in either. Greve and Radda are the usual anchors. Good if you want vineyards and short drives rather than long ones.

Siena, if you would rather not drive

A city small enough to feel like a town, with buses to some of the surrounding villages and organised trips to the rest. The compromise is that you see the region on someone else's schedule.

Getting there and getting around

Which airport

Florence and Pisa are both practical. Pisa often has cheaper fares and is barely further from the southern hill towns; Florence is more convenient if you are starting with a city night.

The car question

For anywhere outside Florence and Siena, the answer is yes. Buses connect the larger towns on limited timetables, but the roads between them — the ones in every photograph of Tuscany — have no service at all. Two or three days with a car is usually enough to see the region properly from a fixed base.

One local quirk worth knowing: most historic centres are ZTL zones, closed to non-resident traffic and camera-enforced. Park outside the walls and walk in. Hotels inside a ZTL can usually register your plate for you, but you have to ask.

Booking the practical bits

  • Rental cars in Italy — pickup at Florence or Pisa airport, free cancellation, rates from around 30 USD a day depending on season and car.
  • Private transfer Pisa airport → Florence — about 1 h 10 and 80 km. Fixed price agreed in advance, driver meets you at arrivals, 90 minutes of free waiting time if the flight is late.
  • Private transfer Florence → Siena — fixed price agreed in advance, driver waiting at arrivals. Sensible if you land late or are travelling with luggage and children.
  • Val d'Orcia day trips — the option if you would rather not drive the hill roads yourself.

What to do from your base

Drive the SP146 between San Quirico and Pienza in the last hour before sunset. It is the road in the photographs, and the light does most of the work.

Take a wine tasting seriously in Montalcino. Brunello is the reason the town exists, and small producers outside the walls usually give a better tasting than the ones on the main square.

Give Pienza an evening, not an afternoon. The day-trip coaches leave around six and the town becomes a different place.

Skip the Leaning Tower queue unless you specifically want to climb it. The rest of the Piazza dei Miracoli is free to walk through and better.

Travelling with children

Tuscany works well with kids, and the reason is the accommodation. Agriturismi — working farms that rent rooms — almost always have a pool, space to run and somewhere to eat without a reservation, and they often cost less than a hotel room in Florence while having a far better view. Several keep animals, which buys you an hour most mornings.

The other half of it is that the drives are short. Twenty to forty minutes between hill towns is normal, so a day out does not turn into three hours in the car. Pick one base and stay put rather than moving every second night — packing up repeatedly is what actually wears everyone down.

Before you go

An eSIM for Italy is worth having before you land — navigation on back roads matters more here than in a city, and you will be reading maps constantly. Travel cover is the other fifteen-minute job.
